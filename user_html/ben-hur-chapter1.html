Chapter 1

The Desert Meeting

The Jebel es Zubleh is a mountain range extending from the mountains of the Hauran to the borders of the desert. The sun was going down behind it, and the long shadows of the peaks were reaching out over the plain. In the midst of the plain, where the shadows were deepest, a man was sitting on a rock, his face turned toward the west, watching the sunset.

He was an old man, with a long white beard and a face that was deeply lined with the marks of thought and sorrow. He wore the simple garments of a traveler, and by his side lay a staff and a small bundle. His name was Balthasar, and he was an Egyptian.

As he sat there, watching the light fade from the sky, he heard a sound behind him. He turned and saw two other men approaching. They were also travelers, and they were leading camels. One was an Indian, dark-skinned and grave; the other was a Greek, fair and eager.

They greeted him with the courtesy of the East, and he returned their greeting. Then they sat down beside him, and for a while they were silent, watching the stars come out one by one in the darkening sky.

"Why are you here?" asked the Greek at last.

"I am waiting," said Balthasar.

"For what?"

"For the King."

The Greek and the Indian looked at each other. They had come from far countries, following a star, and they too were waiting for a King. They spoke of the prophecies they had read, and of the hope that was in their hearts.
