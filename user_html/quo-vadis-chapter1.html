Chapter 1

The Meeting

Petronius woke only about midday, and as usual greatly wearied. The evening before he had been at Nero's, and had remained there late, till the orgies had reached their highest point of madness and debauchery. His body, accustomed to all luxuries, was suffering from the effects of the wine and the late hours.

He raised himself on the bed, and rubbing his eyes, looked around with that listless indifference which characterized his features. The sunlight, falling through the purple drapery of the bed, filled the chamber with a rosy twilight. He called his slaves, who came in noiselessly, and waited for his orders.

"Let the bath be prepared," said he. "And tell me, has any one inquired for me?"

"The noble Marcus Vinicius came an hour ago," replied the chief of the chamber, "but he would not wait, saying that he would return after the bath."

Petronius went to the bath, where he spent a long time in the tepid water, surrounded by perfumes and the soft hands of his attendants. When he returned to his chamber, refreshed and dressed, he found Vinicius waiting for him. Vinicius was a young man of powerful build, with a face that spoke of both courage and a certain youthful impetuosity.

"Greetings, Petronius," said Vinicius. "I have come to you for advice, for you are the wisest man in Rome, and the most skilled in the ways of the world."

"And what is the trouble, Marcus?" asked Petronius, smiling. "Have you fallen in love again? Or perhaps you have lost money at the dice?"

"It is love," said Vinicius. "But not the kind you think. I have met a girl, a hostage from the Lygians, who lives in the house of Aulus Plautius. Her name is Lycia. She is the most beautiful creature I have ever seen, and I must have her."

Petronius looked at him with a mixture of amusement and curiosity. "A Lygian hostage? And in the house of the good Aulus, who is as old-fashioned as a Roman of the early Republic? You have chosen a difficult path, my friend."
