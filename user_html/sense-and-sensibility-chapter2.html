Chapter 2

The New Mistress of Norland

Mrs. John Dashwood now installed herself mistress of Norland; and her mother and sisters-in-law were degraded to the condition of visitors. As such, however, they were treated by her with quiet civility by her husband, who was not a bad-hearted man, but who was a little cold and selfish. He had promised his father to do everything in his power for the comfort of his step-mother and sisters, and he intended to keep his promise.

His wife, however, had no intention of giving away any of her wealth. She thought that her husband's promise was a very foolish one, and she determined to persuade him to break it. She was a strong-minded woman, with a great deal of self-command, and she knew exactly how to manage her husband.

One day, when they were talking about the Dashwood family, Mrs. John Dashwood said: "I am sure I do not know what your father could mean by making such a promise. He had no right to expect you to give away your money to your half-sisters. They are nothing to you, and they have no claim upon you."

"But my father asked me to do it," said Mr. Dashwood. "He asked me to provide for them, and I promised to do it."

"Yes," replied his wife, "but you did not promise to give them a fortune. You promised to do something for them. I think you have done enough for them already. They have had a home here, and they have been treated with every kindness."

"But they will have nothing to live on when they leave Norland," said Mr. Dashwood.

"They will have their mother's fortune," said Mrs. John Dashwood. "That is quite enough for them. They do not need your money."

"I think I should do something for them," said Mr. Dashwood, doubtfully.

"I think you should not," said his wife. "They have no right to expect it, and I am sure they do not deserve it. Let them go and live in a small house somewhere, and be thankful for what they have."

And so, by degrees, Mrs. John Dashwood persuaded her husband to reduce his promised help to a mere nothing, and the Dashwood ladies were left to shift for themselves.
