Chapter 3

Mr. Lloyd's Visit

I was not aware of what I was doing, or where I was, when I woke from my swoon. I found myself supported by some one, and looking up, I saw Mr. Lloyd, the apothecary, who had been sent for. I felt a strange, sickening sensation, and my head swam. I was in the nursery, and Bessie was holding me.

"Where am I?" I asked.

"In the nursery," said Bessie. "You have been ill, Jane; you have been very ill."

Mr. Lloyd was a good-natured man, and he spoke kindly to me. He asked me how I felt, and I told him I was very weak and very sad. He looked at me gravely, and then he turned to Mrs. Reed, who was standing by the door.

"She will be better in the morning," he said. "But she must be kept quiet. She has had a severe shock."

"She has been very naughty," said Mrs. Reed. "She fought with her cousin, and she was very violent."

"That may be," said Mr. Lloyd, "but she is a child, and she has been frightened. I should like to speak to you about her, Mrs. Reed, if you will allow me."

They went out of the room, and I was left alone with Bessie. She was kinder to me now than she had ever been before. She gave me some tea, and she sat by my bed until I fell asleep.

The next day, Mr. Lloyd came again. He asked me if I would like to go to school. I said I would, for I thought that any place would be better than Gateshead. He said he would speak to Mrs. Reed about it, and he did.
