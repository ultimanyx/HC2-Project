Chapter 2

The Star in the East

The three men sat together in the desert night, talking of the star. They had each seen it in their own country, and each had known that it was a sign. The Greek had studied the stars, and he said that it was a new star, one that had never been seen before. The Indian said that it was the star of a great King, foretold by the wise men of his land.

Balthasar said nothing. He was the oldest of the three, and he had been waiting for many years. He had brought gifts with him: gold, frankincense, and myrrh. He had kept them safe through many dangers, and he would not give them up until he had found the King.

As the night wore on, the star grew brighter and brighter. It seemed to hang low in the sky, directly over a place far to the west. The three men watched it in silence, and then they rose and went to their camels.

They journeyed through the night, following the star. The desert was cold and silent, but they did not feel the cold. They were filled with a strange joy, a hope that they could not put into words. They knew that they were going to see something wonderful.

As the dawn broke, they saw a small town in the distance. It was Bethlehem, the city of David. The star stood still over it, and the three men knew that their journey was at an end.
