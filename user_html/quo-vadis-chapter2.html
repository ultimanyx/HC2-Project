Chapter 2

The House of Aulus

Vinicius, having left the house of Petronius, made his way directly to the house of Aulus Plautius. The day was bright and warm, and the streets of Rome were filled with a noisy multitude. But Vinicius saw nothing and heard nothing. He was lost in thought, his mind entirely occupied with the image of Lycia.

The house of Aulus Plautius was an old-fashioned Roman dwelling, simple and severe in its architecture, unlike the luxurious palaces of the newer nobles. It was here that the conqueror of Britain lived, a man respected by all for his virtue and his old Roman simplicity.

Vinicius was admitted by the doorkeeper, and found Aulus in the garden, walking with his wife Pomponia. They greeted him kindly, for Vinicius was a kinsman of Pomponia. But Vinicius, though he tried to be courteous, could scarcely keep his eyes from searching for Lycia.

"You have come at a good hour, Marcus," said Aulus. "The day is fine, and we are about to sit down to our midday meal. You will join us, of course."

Vinicius accepted the invitation eagerly, hoping that Lycia would be present. He was not disappointed. When they entered the dining chamber, he saw her sitting beside Pomponia. She was dressed in a simple white robe, and her hair was bound with a fillet. She was more beautiful than he had remembered.

During the meal, Vinicius tried to speak to her, but she answered only in monosyllables, and her eyes were downcast. He felt a strange mixture of desire and frustration. He was a Roman noble, accustomed to having what he wanted, and this girl's coldness only inflamed his passion.

After the meal, Aulus took Vinicius aside. "Marcus," he said, "I see how you look at Lycia. I must tell you that she is a Christian. She has been baptized, and she follows the teachings of the Galilean. If you wish to marry her, you must know that she will not consent to a union with a pagan."

Vinicius was taken aback. A Christian! He had heard of this strange sect, which was said to worship a crucified criminal and to practice secret rites. He had never given them much thought, but now he felt that this was an obstacle he could overcome. He would have Lycia, whatever the cost.
