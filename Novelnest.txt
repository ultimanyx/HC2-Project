TITLE:
The Villainess's Survival Guide to Dark Romance

Addional Title: 
The book that Bleed

GENRE:
Main Genre: Romance

SYNOPSIS:
Vivien was an avid reader with a passionate hatred for Viella, the foolish villainess in the bestselling dark mafia romance novel Under The Mafia's Obsession. After leaving one too many bitter online comments critiquing Viella’s suicidal stupidity, Vivien suffers a sudden, fatal headache—only to wake up inside the story itself.

The bad news? She is now Viella, the rich, arrogant heiress destined to be murdered by her terrifying mafia boss fiancé, Dante Valerio Moretti.

The worse news? She hasn't arrived at the beginning of the story. The plot is already halfway through: the fragile female lead, Alina, has already been bought by Dante, and the original Viella has already tormented her mercilessly. Tonight is the infamous banquet scene—the turning point where Viella is slated to slap Alina, cementing Dante's lethal hatred and kicking off the countdown to her own gruesome death.

Desperate to avoid her grisly fate, Vivien abandons the villainess lifestyle, swapping sharp weapons for pink ribbons and dramatic revenge for cozy home decor. Clad in the dullest gray dress she can find and armed with fake glasses, she sets out with a simple strategy: act like a background extra, eat free food, and stay completely invisible.

However, in a world driven by obsessive dark romance logic, trying too hard to be ignored might just be the exact thing that draws the deadly mafia don't attention...

CHAPTER 1: THE BOOK THAT BLED
============================================================

NO ONE'S POV

Vivien had always loved dark romance novels, especially the mafia ones. When she picked up the bestseller Under The Mafia's Obsession, she spent days devouring every single word and page.

The story followed Alina, a fragile girl sold by her family to the cruel mafia boss, Dante Valerio Moretti. At first, Dante treated her like property. But slowly, he fell into a mad, terrifying obsession with her.

The biggest wall between their love was the villainess, Viella. She was a rich, proud heiress who emotionally tortured Alina whenever she got the chance. Eventually, Dante killed Viella with his own hands to protect his "little bird."

Vivien hated Viella. She hated her so much that she spent her time leaving comments everywhere online, bashing the character's stupidity.

Before falling asleep, she muttered to herself, "If I were her, I'd never be so stupid. I'd stay away from the leads and avoid getting killed."

Suddenly, a sharp pain stabbed through her head.

Then, darkness.

The first thing Vivien noticed was the warmth. It wasn't the cozy warmth of her small apartment bed.

Her eyelids fluttered open.

She froze.

The ceiling above her was high, decorated with rich people's taste. The bed curtains were pure silk which she definitely couldn't afford. She sat up slowly, her heart pounding.

"What happened? Did I really die? Am I in heaven? Though, looking back, I don't think I did enough good deeds to be here."

She looked around. This wasn't her room. This wasn't her bed. Then, a mirror came into view.

This wasn't her face either.

"Wait... why does this face look so familiar?"

Shiny bright red hair, flawless milk-like skin, golden eyes, rosy round lips...

The realization hit her. This was the face of the cruel villainess who died miserably at the hands of Dante Valerio Moretti.

This was the world of Under The Mafia's Obsession, the book she had finished just last night—or you could say the moment before she died. This wasn't fiction anymore. She was living in it, trapped in the body of the fiancée meant to die.

Her mouth went dry. No. This couldn't be happening. She tried to remember the plot.

"I can still survive, right? I still have time..."

Then, the memories of the original body crashed down. This wasn't the beginning of the book. She was already halfway through the plot.

Alina, the female lead, was already sold to Dante. And this body—her new identity—had already bullied her.

"Shit."

Her stomach twisted. She had already ruined her chances. There would be no redemption arc. The villainess had already abused and humiliated Alina many times. And tonight... tonight was the night.


THE PARTY

In the book, this scene was the turning point. Alina would be serving drinks and accidentally spill wine on the villainess's gown. In response, the villainess would slap her in front of everyone.

Dante would storm in, take Alina's side, and look at his fiancée like she was an eyesore. That was the moment his hatred became deadly. The countdown to her murder would begin.

Vivien's breath hitched. No. She wouldn't let it happen. She wouldn't touch Alina. She wouldn't even look at her. She would change everything.

But first—she had to see what she was working with.

Vivien threw back the covers and slid off the bed. Even the carpet was soft beneath her feet. She walked to the wardrobe. Inside were dozens of gowns and jewels. It was too shiny and colorful. Also, the smell of expensive perfumes hit her nostrils. It made her head hurt.

Then, a knock came at the door. "Miss Viella," a maid called tremblingly. "May we come in to prepare you?"

Vivien remembered the book. Viella was a nightmare to her staff. If she became nice suddenly, they would be suspicious. She had to act like her, at least a bit.

"Enter," she said.

Three maids walked in, heads bowed. They held a blood-red gown covered in crystals. They were shaking so hard that the fabric rustled. One maid named Sarah noticed that and her face became pale.

Vivien looked at the dress and then at the maids. "You're trembling," she said quietly. "Why?"

Sarah nearly tripped over her own feet, her eyes darting to the floor. "I-I'm sorry, Miss! Please don't punish us!"

The other two maids squeezed their eyes shut, waiting for a slap or a scream. But those never came. Instead...

Vivien took the heavy gown and dropped it onto the floor. The maids gasped. Sarah fell to her knees, reaching for the fabric. "I'll clean it! I'll fix it, Miss!"

"Leave it," Vivien said coolly. "This is an embarrassment. I want everything in this room changed. The clothes, the colors, the furniture. Tell the butler to replace it all within the week."

The maids looked at each other in shock.

"Y-yes, Miss," one of the maids said, her voice shaking with relief.

"Start with the bedspread," Vivien added, her eyes sharp. "And Sarah, stand up. I don't like looking at the top of your head."

The maids scrambled to follow her orders, moving faster.

Once they were gone, Vivien sank into a chair. Her hands were shaking. She had to escape this world, but first, she had to survive the ball tonight. She would avoid the female lead and stay away from Dante. She would be invisible.

When the time was right, she would run.

CHAPTER 2: I'LL JUST PRETEND I DON'T EXIST
============================================================

NO ONE'S POV

The mansion didn't look like a villainess's home anymore.

The hallways used to be full of huge gold and silver decor, but now they were replaced with simple, cozy accents. The scary portraits of her ancestors were gone. In their place, Vivien hung paintings of sleepy cats, forests, and a weird frog playing a violin. She liked that frog a lot; it made her feel peaceful.

She got rid of the red carpets and put down soft, fluffy ones. The house went from a dark dungeon to a cozy home. She even changed the kitchen, swapping the fancy silver for wooden spoons. On the table, she kept a fruit basket.

Vivien was finally decorating the house exactly how she had always dreamed of.

She even went into the weapons room and changed things there. Instead of leaving the guns the way they were, she decorated them. She put cute stickers on the handles and tied little pink ribbons around the barrels. To her, they looked much better that way.

The guards and the maids were totally confused. They thought she had lost her mind. One butler even cried when she moved a giant dragon statue to the basement to make room for a bookshelf full of romance novels.

But no one dared to say anything. They were too afraid to ask why their lady, who used to be so violent, was now just sipping tea, reading books, and putting ribbons on weapons.

Vivien took a bite of toast and sighed. Everything was perfect.

Or so she thought—

"Peace," she whispered, looking at a vase of daisies. Just as she was about to smell the flowers—

BAM.

The door slammed open and a maid ran in.

"Lady Viella!!"

Vivien nearly inhaled her toast. "WHAT NOW?!" she yelled, then started coughing hard.

"T-The party!" the maid panted.

Vivien blinked. "Party?"

"Lord Dante's party!" the maid wailed. "It's today!"

Vivien's brain froze. The toast she was holding fell down.

That party! It was the worst event in the whole book.

"No. It can't be possible!!!" Vivien whispered. "I thought since it was postponed, I didn't have to go anymore. That was supposed to be my lucky break!"

It had been a week since she woke up in this world. She had been so busy decorating the house that she had completely forgotten about the plot. She was too focused on her new home.

"It's back on," the maid said, holding up a black invitation. "Tonight."

Vivien jumped out of her seat.

"I'm not ready! I haven't practiced my role yet! I didn't even make any plans, duhh!!!"

As usual, the maid looked at her as if she were speaking an ancient language.

Vivien clutched her shawl. "This is such a betrayal. God, you are really testing me."

She had been so happy with her unlimited money and unlimited moisturizers that she forgot the drama that was waiting for her ahead. Now, she only had a few hours to get ready for a night she was sure would be a disaster.

"I got an idea," Vivien said suddenly. "Tell them I'm sick."

"...Sick?" The maid looked at her, confused.

"Yes!" Vivien pointed dramatically. "Tell them I have a rare disease. Something mysterious, like... emotional tuberculosis."

The maid blinked. "That's not even a real thing."

"It is now."

"Miss, you know your family won't let you skip this. It's too important."

Vivien narrowed her eyes. "What if I pretend to faint? Or maybe hospitalize myself?"

"No," the maid said flatly. "They'd just carry your body into the car and prop you up with sunglasses on."

"...They would, wouldn't they."

"They did it to your cousin Gio when he faked being sick," the maid nodded.

Vivien flopped back into her chair. "Of course they did. This family is too crazy."

She stared at the ceiling for a second. Then, she sat up with a look of determination.

"Fine. If I can't lie, I'll prepare myself instead."

"Prepare how?" the maid asked.

"I will blend in at the party," Vivien declared, walking towards her closet. "I'll wear the dullest colors ever. I'll become a background character like those people in books, you know."

She yanked out a plain gray dress and some flat shoes.

"I will just ignore everyone, focus on eating yummy foods, and if anyone tries to talk to me, I will just pretend I don't know how to talk."

"My lady, you can't do that. It's Lord Dante's party. You're his fiancée! You are definitely going to catch everyone's attention."

Vivien looked at her boring gray dress and smirked. "Not when they don't even recognize me."

The maid sighed and followed her, knowing there was no stopping her now.

Later that night, Vivien threw on a pair of glasses.

Checking her reflection: "I totally look unrecognizable now. No one can know my identity, yeah."

The maid watched with total concern as Vivien started applying the world's most boring lipstick. She intentionally skipped the fancy powders and liners, wanting her face to look as flat as possible. She even messed up her hair slightly, hoping to look untidy. Instead, it gave her an effortless messy-bun look.

She looked down at her dress. It was a simple, gray silky fabric with no lace and a high neckline that covered everything. It fit her body well.

"I don't want to be important tonight. I want to be ignored. Just enough to stay alive..."

"Ah, my lady," the maid started, looking at how the soft gray color actually made her lady's skin and eyes stand out. "The dress is plain, but it makes you look very—"

"I know. Very ugly. Now shh, let me be ugly in peace," Vivien snapped, cutting her off.

As the car was being prepared, Vivien grabbed her purse and got ready.

"I will not talk. I will not slap anyone. I will just sip juice quietly and chew small food... or maybe big, hehe."

She walked out of the room dramatically.

Behind her, the maids whispered. "But she looks even more gorgeous like this. The plain clothes match her so well. I've never seen her like this before."

Vivien didn't listen. She was too busy convincing herself she looked like a background extra.

As she got into her expensive black car, something inside her stirred. She was officially going to meet the man she had only read about in books. The one who was supposed to kill her.

She took a deep breath.

Tonight she was walking into one of the most important scenes in the novel. The one where she was supposed to be hated and humiliated. But maybe... if she kept her head down and avoided the male lead's intense eyes, she could survive.

She looked out the window and whispered, "Vivien... I mean, Viella. You gotta do it. Just get over it tonight and survive, I guess."

And yet, fate had other plans awaiting her.

CHAPTER 3: THE BUFFET STRATEGY
============================================================

NO ONE'S POV

The grand hall of the Moretti estate was breathtaking, dripping in crystal chandeliers, dark mahogany paneling, and an overwhelming scent of rich cigars and expensive champagne. Men in tailored black tuxedos laughed with women draped in dazzling diamonds.

And then, there was Viella.

Or rather, Vivien disguised as an aggressive wallflower.

She walked in through the heavy double doors, intentionally slouching her shoulders just a tiny bit to cut down her noble posture. Her eyes were hidden behind round, wire-framed glasses with plain glass, and her bright red hair was tied back in a loose, frizzy bun.

Alright, Vivien thought to herself, scanning the massive room. Step one: avoid high-traffic areas. Step two: locate the target.

The target wasn't Dante Valerio Moretti.

The target was the three-tiered chocolate fountain by the food table.

Keeping her back against the wall, Vivien stealthily shuffled past groups of whispering aristocrats. She grabbed a small white plate and began loading it up with mini tartlets, smoked salmon rolls, and fried cheese balls.

If I’m going to die in this book eventually, I’m at least going to die full, she reasoned, popping a fried cheese ball into her mouth. Mmm, high-society cheese hits different.

"Is that... Lady Viella?" a quiet whisper drifted from a nearby group of noblewomen.

"No, couldn't be. Viella would never wear gray. She'd rather die than be seen without rubies."

"And look at her shoes! Those are plain flats! Viella wears three-inch heels to step on people's toes."

Vivien let out a low, victorious smirk behind a mouthful of pastry. Ha! It worked! My gray stealth armor is completely impenetrable!

Suddenly, the chatter in the ballroom died down into a suffocating silence.

The heavy doors at the top of the grand staircase swept open.

Vivien froze with a mini tart halfway to her lips. She didn't even need to look up to know who it was. The aura of cold, suffocating pressure filling the room was unmistakable.

Dante Valerio Moretti walked down the stairs.

He looked exactly as the book described—devastatingly handsome, towering, with sharp obsidian eyes, dark wavy hair slicked back, and a tailored black suit that screamed danger. Every movement he made was smooth, deliberate, and terrifyingly calm. Beside him walked a trembling, wide-eyed girl in a simple maid's uniform, clutching a silver serving tray.

Alina. The female lead.

Vivien swallowed hard, crouching slightly behind a large potted palm tree near the buffet table.

There they are, she thought, her heart hammering against her ribs. The main couple. The tyrant don and his tragic little bird. Stay away, Vivien. Don't even breathe in their direction.

As Dante reached the main floor, several high-ranking mob bosses rushed over to greet him. Alina stumbled behind him, her hands trembling so badly that the glasses on her tray clinked together loudly.

In the novel, this was the exact moment. Alina was supposed to walk past Viella, lose her footing on Viella's long gown, spill red wine all over her, and get violently slapped.

Vivien looked down at her gray dress. It barely touched the tops of her flat shoes. Can't trip on a dress that doesn't drag on the floor! Modern problems require modern solutions!

To be extra safe, Vivien backed away another three steps, burying herself deeper into the shadow of the potted plant.

However, fate had a twisted sense of humor.

As Alina backed away nervously from a loud mafioso, she hit the corner of the buffet table. Her heel caught on the edge of the tablecloth.

"Ah!" Alina gasped, her balance giving out completely.

The silver tray slipped from her fingers, sending a full glass of deep red wine flying straight through the air—and heading directly toward Vivien's face.

NOT THE FACE! Vivien screamed internally.

Instead of ducking dramatically or screaming like a proper villainess, Vivien did what any sensible girl terrified for her life would do. She snatched the large silver platter lid from the roast beef section next to her and popped it up like a shield.

CLANG!

The wine glass smashed against the silver lid, splashing red liquid everywhere—mostly on the floor and the metal shield.

The entire ballroom went dead silent.

Alina hit the polished floor with a thud, squeezing her eyes shut, gasping in horror. "I-I'm sorry! I'm so sorry! Please spare me!"

Vivien slowly peeked out from behind her meat-platter shield. She looked down at the wine-stained metal, then at Alina shivering on the ground, and finally noticed every single pair of eyes in the room locked onto her.

Including a pair of intense, pitch-black eyes from across the floor.

Dante Valerio Moretti was staring directly at her. His dark brows were slightly furrowed, his expression unreadable, but his eyes locked onto her with terrifying focus.

Vivien's pulse spiked. Abort mission! Code Red! Do not initiate combat!

She quickly put the platter lid back on the table, dusted off her gray dress, and adjusted her fake glasses.

Instead of screaming at Alina or raising her hand, Vivien took a step back, raised both hands in a polite 'peace' gesture, and said in a calm, slightly muffled voice:

"Accidents happen. Good night."

With that, Vivien turned on her heel, snatched two chocolate-covered strawberries from the table, and began walking away as fast as her flat shoes could carry her toward the exit.

Behind her, Dante watched her retreating gray silhouette, his lips twisting into a subtle, dangerous line.

He had known Viella his entire life. She was loud, demanding, and bloodthirsty. But the woman standing before him tonight... clad in a sack of gray silk, holding a roast beef lid like a tactical shield, and offering a 'peace' sign before fleeing with stolen strawberries...

Dante narrowed his eyes, a strange, dark amusement igniting in his cold chest.

"Find out," Dante murmured quietly to his second-in-command standing behind him, "why my fiancée is dressed like a mouse."

CHAPTER 4: THE MOUSE AND THE CAT

NO ONE'S POV

Vivien sprinted into the back seat of her luxury sedan, slamming the door shut with enough force to rattle the tinted windows.

"Drive! Fast! Go go go!" she wheezed, throwing her body across the leather seats and clutching her stolen chocolate strawberries to her chest like precious jewels.

The driver, a stoic old man named Thomas who had worked for the family for twenty years, glanced back in confusion through the rearview mirror. "My lady? Is there an attack? Should I signal the security detail?"

"Worse!" Vivien gasped, stuffing a strawberry into her mouth to calm her panicked heart. "I made eye contact! I held up a platter lid like Captain America and made eye contact with the head devil himself!"

Thomas blinked. "...Captain who, my lady?"

"Just floor it, Thomas!"

As the car roared away from the Moretti estate, Vivien buried her face in her hands, groaning softly. She had done it. She had survived the infamous slap scene. There was no wine spilled on her gray dress, no dramatic altercation, and no reason for Dante to hunt her down for mistreating his sweet "little bird."

Except... the look in his eyes right before she ran away.

In the novel, Dante Valerio Moretti was described as a predator who only paid attention to two things: threats to his empire, and Alina's innocent beauty. He treated Viella like an annoying, loud pest that he tolerated solely for the political alliance between their families—until she pushed him too far and he executed her.

"He looked at me like I was a budget magic trick," Vivien muttered, pushing her wire-rimmed glasses up her nose. "That's fine. Curiosity dies fast. Tomorrow he'll forget all about the gray mouse and go back to obsessed stalking of the female lead."

Satisfied with her logic, Vivien spent the rest of the ride home planning her next move: full isolation. If she stayed inside her cozy, frog-violin-decorated mansion for the next three months, the main plot would naturally resolve itself. Dante would fall hopelessly in love with Alina, break off the engagement with Viella, and Vivien could take her massive settlement money and move to a quiet seaside village in Italy to read books forever.

It was a bulletproof plan.

Meanwhile, inside the dimly lit master study of the Moretti mansion, the atmosphere was suffocating.

Dante sat behind a heavy oak desk, a crystal glass of amber whiskey resting between his long, gloved fingers. On the soft velvet sofa across from him, Alina sat trembling, wrapped in a warm blanket while a maid tended to her scraped knees.

Dante ignored the girl entirely. His cold, obsidian eyes were fixed on the tablet his second-in-command, Leo, had just placed before him.

"Explain," Dante said, his voice a low, smooth baritone that sent chills through the room.

Leo cleared his throat, looking unusually baffled. "Boss... we audited Lady Viella's recent activities as requested. Over the past six days, she has made... radical changes."

"Has she been meeting with rival families? Acquiring unauthorized firearms? Plotting against the Moretti line?"

"No, sir," Leo replied, swiping to the next slide on the screen. "She ordered thirty-four plush rugs, fired her head torturer, bought twenty-two pastel throw pillows, and replaced the portraits of your late ancestors with paintings of amphibians playing musical instruments."

Dante paused, his whiskey glass halting halfway to his lips. "Amphibians."

"A frog playing a violin, specifically, sir. Furthermore..." Leo pulled up a financial receipt. "She ordered two customized pink ribbons and attached them to her customized gold-plated Beretta 9mm."

A heavy silence descended upon the study.

Dante set his glass down on the desk with a soft clack. He leaned back in his leather chair, steepling his fingers. In all the years he had known Viella—the proud, arrogant heiress forced upon him by political convenience—she had been predictable. She craved status, wore loud jewelry, screamed at servants, and clung to his arm whenever press cameras were nearby.

Tonight, she had worn a dress that looked like a dishcloth, hid behind a palm tree to eat fried cheese, guarded herself with a meat lid, and wished him 'peace' before running away in flats.

"Is she sick?" Dante asked quietly.

"She claimed to her head butler that she suffers from 'emotional tuberculosis,' sir," Leo reported dutifully, though his voice twitched slightly. "Medical records show no such illness exists."

Dante stared at the photo on the screen—a security camera snapshot of Viella holding the silver tray lid, her eyes behind round glasses looking wide, frantic, and oddly hilarious.

A slow, dangerous smirk tugged at the corner of Dante's sharp lips.

"Emotional tuberculosis," Dante murmured. "How intriguing."

He stood up, adjusting his dark suit jacket. Alina flinched at the sudden movement, but Dante didn't even cast a glance in her direction as he walked toward the floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the estate grounds.

"Cancel my morning meetings tomorrow, Leo," Dante ordered smoothly.

Leo blinked. "Sir? You have a distribution meeting with the Sicilian cartel at ten."

"Reschedule it," Dante said, his dark eyes glittering with a predatory spark. "I think it's time I pay a visit to my fiancée. I must express my deep condolences... for her terrible illness."

CHAPTER 5: UNANNOUNCED VISITOR

NO ONE'S POV

The morning sun filtered brightly through the pastel sheer curtains of the main estate salon.

Vivien was having the time of her life. She was wearing a giant, oversized fuzzy yellow sweater, plaid pajama pants, and thick fuzzy socks with little bear ears on the toes. She was sprawled across her brand-new velvet sofa, a mug of hot cocoa in one hand and a high-stakes romance novel in the other.

"Ah, bliss," Vivien sighed, taking a sip of whipped cream. "No mafia violence, no crazy yandere male leads, just pure, unadulterated comfort."

"M-My lady!"

Sarah, the young maid, burst into the salon, her face so pale she looked like a ghost that had seen another, scarier ghost.

Vivien didn't even look up from her book. "Sarah, what did we say about breaking the cozy energy of the room? Take three deep breaths and tell me if the bakery delivered the cinnamon rolls."

"It's not the bakery, Miss!" Sarah squeaked, her knees knocking together. "He's here!"

"Who's here? The curtain installer?"

"Lord Dante!"

Pfft!

Vivien sprayed hot cocoa across her fluffy blanket. She choked violently, coughing until her chest heaved, her eyes wide with unadulterated horror.

"WHO?!" she shrieked, her voice cracking.

"Lord Dante Valerio Moretti!" Sarah wailed softly. "He's in the foyer! He brought four armed men and he's walking up the stairs right now!"

"Why is he here?!" Vivien scrambled off the sofa like a frantic cat, tossing her book into the air. "The plot doesn't say he visits her house! He never visits her house! He hates her house!"

"I don't know, Miss! What do we do?!"

"Hide me! Put me in the laundry basket! Tell him I died of the emotional disease!"

Before Vivien could dive behind the sofa, heavy, measured footsteps echoed down the grand hallway. The double oak doors of the salon swung open smoothly.

The air in the room instantly plummeted by ten degrees.

Dante Valerio Moretti stepped into the pastel-pink, cat-picture-adorned salon.

He was dressed in a crisp, dark charcoal suit, his dark hair immaculate, his presence so imposing that the cute fluffy furniture seemed to shrink in fear. Behind him stood two massive, stone-faced bodyguards in dark sunglasses.

Dante stopped in the middle of the room. His sharp, pitch-black eyes slowly swept over the surroundings—taking in the painting of the violin-playing frog, the fluffy rugs, the pink ribbons tied around a vase, and finally, settling on the girl standing frozen in the middle of the carpet.

She was wearing a yellow sweater that swallowed her whole, hair tied up in a messy nest, holding a stained napkin, staring at him with wide, terrified golden eyes.

Silence stretched across the room.

Vivien's heart was hammering so fast she thought it might shatter her ribs. Why is he here? Did he realize I'm an imposter? Did I ruin the plot so badly that he came to murder me early?!

Dante took a slow step forward. Vivien instinctively took a step back.

Dante stopped, a faint, amused tilt appearing on his lips.

"Good morning, Viella," Dante said, his voice smooth as velvet and cold as ice. "I heard you were gravely ill. I came to see if you needed a doctor... or a priest."

Vivien swallowed hard, her mind racing at a million miles per hour. She had to act. She had to protect her background-extra status, or at least convince him she was too pathetic to be worth killing.

She cleared her throat, pulled her oversized sweater down to cover her hands, and lowered her head, putting on her best weak, fragile voice.

"A-Ah... Lord Dante," Vivien stammered, coughing weakly into her sleeve. "How... how kind of you. As you can see... my condition is quite dire. The doctor says I must refrain from all social interactions, mafia politics, and... fiancé duties for at least six to eight years."

Dante raised an eyebrow. He walked closer, stepping around a cute pink pouf, until he was standing directly in front of her. He was so tall that his shadow completely engulfed her.

He reached out a gloved hand. Vivien squeezed her eyes shut, preparing for the chokehold—

Instead, Dante gently plucked a tiny piece of whipped cream off her upper lip with his gloved thumb.

Vivien's eyes snapped open.

"Your illness," Dante murmured, leaning down slightly so his dark eyes were level with hers, "seems to smell strongly of chocolate and marshmallow."

Vivien's cheeks flushed bright red. "It's... medicinal chocolate. For the soul."

Dante let out a low, dark chuckle—a sound that sent a strange, dangerous shiver down Vivien's spine.

"Is that so?" Dante murmured, his eyes scanning her bright red hair and rosy cheeks. "You seem remarkably different, my dear. Tell me... where is the girl who threatened to break my maid's legs last week?"

Vivien gulped. "She... she retired. I am her peaceful twin sister now."

Dante's eyes narrowed, a sharp, dangerous intrigue gleaming in the dark depths. "Fascinating. Because the girl in front of me... is infinitely more dangerous."

CHAPTER 6: THE TEA PARTY OF TERROR

NO ONE'S POV

"Dangerous?!" Vivien squeaked, her voice flying an octave higher than normal. "Me? Dangerous? Lord Dante, I literally just spent twenty minutes trying to open a jar of jam and gave up to cry on the floor!"

Dante didn't answer right away. He simply walked past her, taking a seat on her plush velvet sofa. The terrifying mafia don sat comfortably among pastel throw pillows, crossing one long leg over the other, looking completely out of place yet utterly in command of the room.

"Sit," Dante commanded gently, gesturing to the armchair across from him.

Vivien hesitated, then shuffled over, sitting on the very edge of the cushion like a bomb squad technician handling an active explosive.

"Sarah," Vivien whispered without looking away from Dante. "Tea. Please. Plain black tea. No poison, I promise."

"Right away, Miss!" Sarah squeaked, ducking out of the room like a rabbit fleeing a hawk.

Dante leaned back, resting his chin on his gloved hand. His dark eyes tracked every tiny twitch of Vivien's face. "You haven't called me 'my dark prince' once since I arrived."

Vivien almost gagged. My dark prince?! Did the original Viella actually call him that?! Gross!

"Ah... yes," Vivien said smoothly, adjusting her messy bun. "I realized that titles are a construct of social vanity. 'Lord Dante' is perfectly functional. Or 'Sir'. Or 'Hey you'."

One of the bodyguards standing by the door let out a muffled gasp. Dante raised a single hand, silencing the guard instantly, though his own expression remained locked in a state of intense fascination.

"You skipped the annual gala's main event last night," Dante said, his voice dropping into a colder, more professional tone. "And you didn't touch Alina."

Vivien straightened her back, her protective instincts flaring. Here it was. The real test. He was testing her to see if she was still a threat to his precious female lead.

"Alina is a very sweet girl," Vivien said quickly, trying to sound as genuine and un-villainous as possible. "She was just doing her job. I hold no ill will toward her. In fact, I think she deserves a raise! And maybe a nice vacation! Away from... well, away from scary places."

Dante's eyes flickered. "You aren't jealous?"

"A family dinner with the Old Don?!" Vivien wailed, clutching a stuffed frog to her chest. "Chapter 13! The strict father-in-law evaluation chapter! This is where the villainess usually gets tested on mafia etiquette and poison identification!"

"My lady," Sarah said, holding up a sleek, elegant black evening gown. "Lord Dante sent this dress for you. He requested you wear something... formal."

Vivien looked at the black dress. It was stunning—simple, elegant, with a high slit and a low back. It wasn't gray, and it wasn't a neon yellow canary suit. It was the dress of a true mafia queen.

"He wants me to look the part," Vivien realized, her eyes narrowing. "He wants me to act like a proper heiress so his scary father doesn't replace me."

Vivien smirked. An opportunity!

If Don Carmine hated her, he would force Dante to cancel the engagement! Dante couldn't disobey the Old Don without starting a civil war within his own family!

"Sarah," Vivien grinned evilly. "Put the black dress back. We're going full chaos mode."

At seven o'clock, the grand dining hall of the Moretti Manor was heavy with tension.

Don Carmine sat at the head of the long mahogany table—a imposing, white-haired man with cold blue eyes, flanked by four senior mob captains. Dante sat to his right, looking stoic and calm.

The heavy double doors swept open.

The butler announced, "Lady Viella of the House of Vance."

Vivien walked in.

Dante's eyes widened slightly. Don Carmine's jaw tightened.

Vivien was wearing the sleek black dress Dante had sent—but she had heavily modified it.

Over the elegant silk dress, she was wearing a bright pink apron with the words "KISS THE COOK" printed in bold white letters. In her hands, instead of a designer clutch, she was carrying a Tupperware container filled with homemade chocolate chip cookies.

"Good evening, everyone!" Vivien announced cheerful, bouncing into the terrifying mafia dining hall like a golden retriever at a funeral. "Sorry I'm late! The cookies took extra time to bake!"

Don Carmine stared at her, his cold blue eyes practically bulging out of his head. "What... what is the meaning of this insolence?"

Vivien walked right up to the Old Don, popping open the Tupperware lid and thrusting it into his face.

"Cookie, Don Carmine?" Vivien offered sweetly. "They're gluten-free! I know old people have digestive issues!"

One of the senior captains gasped, clutching his chest in horror.

Don Carmine stood up, slamming his heavy hands onto the table, his face turning bright red with rage. "DANTE! Is this the foolish girl you intend to marry?! She dresses like a housemaid and speaks to me of digestion?!"

Vivien cheered internally. YES! IT'S WORKING! REJECT ME! BANISH ME TO ITALY!

Carmine glared at Vivien, his voice booming like thunder. "She has no dignity! She brings baked goods to a war council! Break this engagement immediately!"

Vivien looked over at Dante, waiting for him to bow to his father's command and end her misery.

Dante slowly stood up.

He didn't look angry. He didn't look embarrassed.

Instead, Dante reached out, took a chocolate chip cookie from Vivien's Tupperware container, and took a deliberate bite.

"They're delicious, Father," Dante said smoothly, his voice dropping into a dangerous, commanding tone that completely eclipsed his father's anger.

Carmine blinked. "Dante—"

"Viella will be my wife," Dante declared, stepping next to Vivien and wrapping a long, possessive arm around her waist, pulling her close to his side. "She brings cookies instead of poison. She wears pink aprons because she pleases herself. And if anyone in this family has a problem with her..."

Dante's eyes swept over his father and the four terrified captains, burning with lethal authority.

"...they can answer to me."

Silence fell over the dining room.

Don Carmine slowly sank back into his chair, stunned by his son's absolute defiance.

Vivien stood frozen in Dante's firm grip, staring at her cookie in utter defeat.

I gave the Old Don a gluten-free cookie, Vivien thought miserably. AND THE MALE LEAD STILL DEFENDED ME. WHAT IS WRONG WITH THIS FAMILY?!

CHAPTER 14: THE CONFESSION UNDER THE STARS

NO ONE'S POV

The rest of the family dinner had been an uncomfortable blur. Don Carmine hadn't said another word, the captains had eaten their steak in terrified silence, and Dante had casually eaten three more of Vivien's cookies.

Now, Vivien stood on the grand marble balcony of the Moretti manor, looking out over the sprawling, moonlit gardens below. The cool night air blew through her bright red hair, chilling her bare shoulders.

She had taken off the pink apron, leaving her in the sleek black dress.

"You're going to catch a cold."

A heavy, warm coat was gently draped over her shoulders. The scent of cedarwood and dark tobacco instantly surrounded her.

Vivien didn't turn around. She knew it was Dante.

"Why did you do that?" Vivien asked quietly, leaning her elbows against the stone railing. "Your father would have let you break the engagement. You could have been free of me."

Dante walked up beside her, resting his hands on the railing, looking out at the gardens.

"I don't want to be free of you," Dante said softly.

Vivien let out a dry, sad laugh. "Dante, stop playing games. I know what kind of man you are. You're cold, ruthless, and calculating. You don't love people. You own them."

She turned to face him, looking up into his sharp, handsome face illuminated by the moonlight.

"I tried everything to make you hate me," Vivien said, her golden eyes filled with genuine confusion. "I wore ugly clothes. I acted like an accountant. I bought frog paintings. I gave your scary father cookies! Any sane mafia don would have replaced me weeks ago!"

Dante slowly turned his body to face her.

He didn't look angry. He looked... remarkably vulnerable, a sight that made Vivien's heart skip a beat.

"In my entire life," Dante began, his deep voice carrying a rare, raw honesty, "everyone around me has played a role. My father wanted a cold heir. My enemies wanted a monster. The women in high society wanted my title and money."

He reached out, his gloved fingers gently tucking a loose strand of red hair behind her ear.

"And then you woke up one day," Dante murmured, his dark eyes searching hers. "You stopped pretending to care about status. You stopped screaming for power. You looked at me—not as a don, not as a prize—but as a person you genuinely wanted to avoid."

Vivien swallowed hard, her mouth dry. "Because you're dangerous..."

"Because I was boring to you," Dante corrected with a faint, wry smile. "You brought color into a house that was dead, Viella. You protected my maid. You turned my weapons into toys. You made me laugh for the first time since my mother died."

Vivien's breath hitched.

"I don't want a quiet mafia bride who bows to my father," Dante whispered, stepping closer until there was no space left between them. "I want the girl who fights kidnappers in a yellow dress and offers my enemy a pink-ribboned gun."

He leaned down, his forehead gently resting against hers.

"I'm not playing a game, Viella," Dante said, his voice thick with a deep, undeniable passion. "I don't care about the original alliance. I don't care about my father's rules. I love you."

The word hung in the cool night air like a sacred vow.

I love you.

Vivien's brain completely short-circuited.

In the novel Under The Mafia's Obsession, Dante Valerio Moretti had never said those words to Viella. He had only ever said them to Alina, near the very end of the story, after hundreds of pages of angst and bloodshed.

Now, under the starlight, the terrifying male lead was confessing his love to the villainess.

Vivien looked into his dark eyes, seeing the absolute sincerity burning within them. She felt a strange, fluttering warmth bloom inside her chest—a feeling she had been trying to suppress for weeks.

Oh no, Vivien thought, panic and something far more dangerous swirling in her mind. Oh no... I think... I think I'm falling for the male lead.

"Dante..." Vivien whispered.

Before she could finish her sentence, a loud gunshot echoed from the garden below.

CHAPTER 15: THE SURVIVAL GUIDE'S FINAL RULE

NO ONE'S POV

BANG!

A high-caliber sniper bullet shattered the stone balustrade right where Dante's chest had been a fraction of a second before.

In a lightning-fast reflex, Dante grabbed Vivien around the waist and tackled her to the marble floor of the balcony, covering her body with his own as stone dust and debris rained down upon them.

"SNIPER!" Leo's voice roared from inside the manor. "SECURE THE DON!"

Red laser sights danced across the dark balcony walls. Footsteps echoed from the gardens below—Don Carmine's loyalist guards were mounting a coup! The Old Don had decided if Dante wouldn't break the engagement, he would eliminate both of them to take back control of the family!

"Are you hit?!" Dante growled, scanning Vivien's body frantically, his eyes wild with terror.

"I'm... I'm okay!" Vivien wheezed, lying beneath him on the cold marble.

Dante pulled his signature dark pistol from his shoulder holster, his face instantly twisting into a expression of pure, unadulterated fury. "Stay down! I'll kill them all—"

"Wait!" Vivien grabbed his wrist.

Dante looked down at her, surprised.

Vivien looked at the laser sights, then at the shattered balcony, and finally at the man lying over her—the terrifying mafia don who had just confessed his love to her, ready to burn the world down to keep her safe.

She wasn't in the novel Under The Mafia's Obsession anymore. She had rewritten every single page with her own hands.

She was no longer just Vivien, the reader trying to survive. She was Viella—the woman who had tamed the deadliest man in Italy with frog paintings and chocolate chip cookies.

A slow, confident, villainess smirk spread across Vivien's lips.

"Rule Number One of the Survival Guide," Vivien said softly, her golden eyes flashing with a dangerous new fire.

Dante blinked. "What?"

Vivien reached into her sleek black evening dress thigh-holster—where she had hidden her custom weapon under her skirt before dinner.

She pulled out her gold-plated Beretta 9mm, the bright pink ribbons fluttering in the night breeze.

"When someone tries to ruin your cozy home," Vivien purred, clicking the safety off with a sharp CLICK, "you don't run away. You shoot back."

Dante stared at her for one second. Then, a dark, breathless grin broke across his handsome face.

"God," Dante whispered, his voice dripping with pure adoration. "I love you so much."

"Save the romance for later, Dark Prince," Vivien winked, rolling out from under him and taking cover behind the stone pillar. "Cover me!"

Vivien popped up from behind the pillar, aimed her pink-ribboned pistol into the darkness of the garden, and pulled the trigger.

BANG!

A sniper dropped from the trees below with a loud thud.

Dante laughed—a wild, free, dangerous sound—and stepped up beside her, firing his own weapon into the night, fighting side-by-side with his neon-loving, cookie-baking, pink-ribbon-toting mafia queen.

The original plot was dead.

The villainess was alive.

And her dark romance was just beginning.
