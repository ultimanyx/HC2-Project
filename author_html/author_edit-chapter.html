The night House Veyra fell, the sky above Kaldros turned the colour of old blood. Mara had been asleep when the horns first sounded — long, low, and wrong. She would remember that sound for the rest of her life.

By the time she reached the courtyard, the great iron doors had already been breached. Torchlight danced across the faces of men she had known since childhood, men who now wore the crimson sash of the usurper. None of them met her eyes.

She ran. Through corridors she had memorised as a girl, past portraits of ancestors whose names she would one day carry, past the library where her mother had taught her to read. She ran until the screams behind her faded into silence.
